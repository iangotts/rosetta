01

From library to oracle

Phase 1 gets someone to the right video. This phase lets them skip that step entirely and just ask.

Right now, a subscriber has to know roughly what they're looking for — a tag, an expert, a title — and go watch it. The Wisdom Layer inverts that: ask a real coaching question in plain language — "How do I tell my co-founder it's time to step back?" — and get back a synthesized, cited answer drawn from everything every Performance Intelligence expert has ever said on the subject, not just the one video you happened to find.

That's the same leap AquaBrain makes for a person's own notes: from "a pile of things I once wrote down" to "ask anything, get a grounded answer, tagged to a Lens, with the sources shown." This spec applies that exact model to a shared corpus of expert video instead of one person's private notes.

One real difference AquaBrain is a personal Brain — one person's own thoughts, private by default. This is a shared, product-owned corpus built from published expert content, queried by many subscribers. The corpus itself has no per-user privacy boundary — but a subscriber's own questions and saved reflections (section 08) need exactly the privacy AquaBrain gives a person's own thoughts.
03

Ingestion: transcript → Wisdom Fragment

Nothing here replaces Phase 1's YouTube pipeline (section 06 of the base spec) — it runs one step further, on the transcript that pipeline already pulls for search and captions.

1
Transcript
Auto-captions already pulled per-video in Phase 1
2
Segment
An LLM pass finds where the SME shifts from one idea to the next — not fixed time windows
3
Extract
Each segment yields one self-contained Wisdom Fragment, timestamp preserved
4
Classify
Fragment type + Lens tags + SME + a confidence score
5
Admin review
Low-confidence fragments queue for a human spot-check, reusing Phase 1's approval screen
6
Corpus
Approved fragments become searchable

The quality bar is the one already established in Phase 1: Audvisor's T.R.A.C.K. — Timeless, Relevant, Actionable, Curated, Knowledge — applied here to individual fragments, not just whole Insights. Nothing gets quoted back to a subscriber as advice without having passed it.

06

Ask & synthesis

Walking through one real question: "How do I have a succession conversation with my co-founder, who doesn't want to step back?"

  1. Embed and search. The question is embedded and matched against Wisdom Fragments, pre-scoped to Succession Planning and Difficult Conversations.
  2. Rank. Relevance first, with a light authority signal from each SME's own engagement stats (plays/shares per tag, already tracked in Phase 1) as a weak prior — never a hard override.
  3. Compose, grounded only in what was retrieved. The answer is built strictly from matched fragments — never the model's own free-associated coaching advice. This is a hard rule, not a style preference: an "expert-sourced" product that quietly isn't is a trust problem, not just a quality one.
  4. Surface disagreement. If two SMEs meaningfully conflict — one says give an ultimatum, another says never force the timeline — both are shown, not silently resolved. This is the CONTESTED pattern from section 02, applied to disagreeing experts instead of a disagreeing set of one person's own notes.
  5. Cite everything. Every claim carries an SME name, video title, timestamp, and a deep link — the same "Sources:" discipline as any other grounded answer.
  6. Offer a next step. Watch the full clip, save the answer as a Note, or open the Succession Planning Collection for more.

Overcoming Anxiety & Performance Pressure

Skill

Framed explicitly as performance coaching — pre-performance nerves, reframing pressure, routines the experts themselves describe.

Flow
What the pressure shows up as → what's been tried → grounded, cited reframes and routines
Draws on
Overcoming Anxiety & Performance Pressure fragments
Non-negotiable guardrail The Overcoming Anxiety Skill is performance coaching, not therapy, and it says so plainly. It does not diagnose, does not present itself as a substitute for professional mental-health care, and never reaches for content that mimics or normalises self-harm coping. If a subscriber's language suggests real crisis rather than performance pressure, the Skill stops coaching and hands off to crisis resources instead of producing more content. This isn't a caveat to soften later — it's a fixed design requirement from day one, the same standard this assistant already holds itself to.

On pricing The base spec noted Audvisor's $120/year as a loose benchmark for a micro-content subscription. This product isn't equivalent — a synthesized, multi-expert, cited answer is a different value proposition from one 3-minute episode — so it shouldn't be priced off that comp directly. Real pricing needs willingness-to-pay research against this specific offer, not a borrowed number.
11

Rights & consent

This is the single biggest difference from Phase 1, and the biggest risk in this document.

Phase 1 only ever embeds or links to a YouTube video — no re-hosting, safely inside YouTube's Terms of Service. The Wisdom Layer is materially different: it extracts, stores, and resells the substance of what people say, as a queryable subscription product. That most likely needs:

  • Explicit clearance from Jez Cartwright to use transcript content this way — separate from, and beyond, YouTube's embed terms.
  • A separate consent step for guest SMEs. Being interviewed for a free YouTube video is not the same as having your specific words extracted, attributed, and resold as structured advice inside a paid product. Recommend extending Phase 1's SME-approval screen with an explicit "wisdom extraction" consent flag, off by default.
  • Attribution as a requirement, not just good practice. Every fragment must carry a real "said by X in Y" citation — never presented as the product's own generic wisdom.
Blocking, not just a footnote This needs real legal review before build starts, not after. Nothing else in this document should proceed past the ingestion-and-review stage (section 03) until it has one.

13

Open questions

  • Rights clearance (section 11) — blocking; needs resolving before build, not before launch.
  • Minimum corpus size. What counts as "enough" transcripts before "collective wisdom" is credible rather than collective-of-ten?
  • Human sign-off on synthesized answers — does Jez (or a coach on his behalf) review the Ask experience before it's trusted at scale, the way Insights are approved today?
  • Coach-tier pricing — needs real research, not a borrowed number.
  • Tone of disagreement — how does surfacing two conflicting experts stay confident and useful rather than reading as hedging?
  • MCP infrastructure ownership — who hosts and operates the server, and at what cost per subscriber?
14

Self-critique

Solid The AquaBrain mapping in section 02 isn't a loose analogy — this session has live visibility into AquaBrain's actual tool set and Lens/Thought model, so Fragment↔Thought, Coaching Lens↔Lens, CONTESTED, and get_skill↔Coaching Skills are all borrowed from a real, working system, not invented from scratch.
Weaker Everything past ingestion is unbuilt and untested. Auto-captions have real error rates, and an extraction pass can produce a fragment that sounds plausible but wasn't quite what the SME said — the human-review gate in section 03 is a mitigation, not a guarantee, and answer quality needs real validation before this earns subscriber trust.
Weaker The rights question in section 11 is flagged clearly, not glossed over — but I'm not a lawyer, and nothing here substitutes for real legal review of what extracting and reselling transcript substance actually requires.
Held the line I didn't invent a Coach-tier price. Audvisor's number doesn't transfer to a different value proposition, and saying that plainly beats dressing up a guess as a benchmark.
