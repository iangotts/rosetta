01

How to read this

The sequencing and dependencies below are real planning judgement. The week numbers are not.

Durations assume a small team working in parallel — roughly 3–5 full-stack/product engineers, one iOS specialist joining at Phase 1, and a data/ML engineer joining for Phase 3 — with no team-size or velocity data behind that assumption. Treat the timeline as relative sequencing — what can run in parallel, what blocks what — and recalibrate the actual week numbers once real resourcing is known. That's a deliberate choice: inventing a fake calendar would be worse than not having one, the same reason neither prior spec invented a subscription price.

03

Phase 0 — Foundations

Nothing here is a full-time long pole — it's the handful of decisions and one-off audits that everything downstream depends on.

WorkstreamCloses the gap
Legal & rights scopingStarts now because it's slow, not because Phase 1 needs it — it gates Phase 3a, not Phase 0 itself
Naming, pricing tier, iOS-approach decisionsBase spec's open questions — each blocks a specific downstream workstream if left open
YouTube content & catalog auditCloses the base spec's own flagged gap: the real Shorts-vs-long-form split, not an assumed one

Exit criteria: naming and pricing tier decided (even directionally); iOS build approach confirmed; a real catalog count in hand; component library ready for engineering to consume.

05

Phase 2 — Deepen

My Notes, the PDF export reading pack, Messages/inbox, Audvisor-inspired "share to a colleague with a note," and a first pass at tag-affinity recommendations.

Exit criteria: retention and engagement from the Phase 1 beta justify further investment, and there's enough real usage data to prioritise which Coaching Lenses matter most in Phase 3.

07

Phase 4 — Scale & extend

Push notifications for new SME uploads, offline note sync, expanding Coaching Lenses and Skills from real usage rather than the starter set, broader personalization. Drawn hatched on the timeline on purpose — this phase is genuinely unscoped until Phase 3 tells us what subscribers actually ask for.

08

Gates & dependencies

Three points where the plan can't proceed on schedule alone — something specific has to be true first.

GateBlocksMust be true first
APhase 3a startLegal & rights clearance closed — the Wisdom Layer spec's biggest flagged risk (section 11 there)
B3a → 3bCorpus size/quality threshold met, not just "some time has passed"
C3b → 3cIn-app Ask quality validated with real subscribers before it's handed to an external MCP audience
If a gate slips A slipped gate delays only what it blocks. Legal running long pushes 3a, not Phase 1 or 2 — they don't depend on it. That's the point of running legal scoping from week one: it has the most calendar risk and the least engineering dependency, so it should never be the reason engineering is idle.

10

Risks to the plan itself

Not feature risk — already covered in both specs — schedule risk specifically.

  • Legal takes longer than four weeks. Mitigated by starting it in week one and not putting anything else behind it except Gate A.
  • The iOS-approach decision slips. Platform-shell work (week 3) can't really start without it — this is the one Foundations decision with a hard downstream dependency, so it deserves being forced early rather than left open.
  • The content audit reveals a thin Shorts catalog. If the channel turns out to be long-form-heavy, the Discover feed's scope and the Phase 1 timeline itself may need resizing — better to learn this in week one than week eleven.
11

Self-critique

Solid The sequencing and gates are real judgement calls grounded in what each spec already established — Gate A through C aren't new constraints, they're the dependencies both documents already named, made explicit and placed on a timeline.
Weaker, by design The week numbers are illustrative, stated plainly as such in section 01, and built on an undisclosed-elsewhere team-size assumption I made explicit rather than hid. Once real resourcing exists, the bars will move; the order and the gates most likely won't.
Not covered This is a build plan, not a budget — no cost estimates, and no attempt to size the legal engagement itself, which is a real unknown this plan just treats as "start it early."
