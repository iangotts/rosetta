Part 1 · §1.1
Executive Summary

Rosetta is an AI-powered knowledge enablement platform built for the Salesforce ecosystem. It serves as a structured knowledge backbone that ingests authoritative source documents, codifies their semantic content into a graph-based knowledge model, and uses that structured knowledge to guide practitioners — called Trailblazers — through complex implementation decisions.

Phase 1 targets Data 360 and Agentforce. The architecture is deliberately product-agnostic and is intended to scale to any Salesforce product line — and adjacent ecosystem products — over subsequent phases. This generalisation is a load-bearing design assumption, not a future enhancement: the metamodel, graph schema, and retrieval pipeline treat the product surface as data, not code.

The core business insight behind Rosetta is that enterprise software implementation guidance exists across thousands of documents, but accessing the right knowledge at the right moment in a project requires more than keyword search. Rosetta translates business intent into structured architectural patterns and actionable configuration guidance through a conversational AI agent grounded in a semantically-organised knowledge graph.

A second insight, surfaced during the May 2026 review: the most valuable context in any active engagement is not the vendor's published documentation — it is the systems integrator's project artifacts: discovery dossiers, design docs, prior decision records, internal playbooks. Rosetta is designed to ingest and index that material alongside the vendor corpus, so retrieval can reason over both at once.

Core Value Proposition

When a practitioner asks a question about configuring a Salesforce product, Rosetta does not hallucinate an answer. Instead, it traverses a structured graph of validated architectural patterns, identifies the best matching approaches for the practitioner's specific situation, and delivers guidance citing exactly which patterns and which source passages apply, and why.

Access surface

Rosetta is consumed through four access surfaces:

Slack
Primary practitioner surface. Slack workflows and Agentforce agents invoke Rosetta via MCP — meeting Sellers, Implementors, and Engineering in the tool they already use.
Headless via MCP
MCP server exposes Rosetta to Claude.ai, custom agents, and ISV tools (Cuneiform, Elements.cloud, Hubbl). Same tools, same audit ledger, same citation contract as Slack.
REST API
Bearer-token authenticated for direct service-to-service integration. The substrate beneath both Slack and Headless surfaces.
Conversational UI (for internal testing)
Next.js application at rosetta-design.com. Used by the Rosetta team for corpus replay, behavioural assertion, and reviewer walkthroughs.
Part 1 · §1.2
Business Problem

Rosetta addresses a specific set of ease-of-use problems for the Salesforce ecosystem identified through the Keenan Vision research engagement with Salesforce. The research surfaced five categories of friction affecting customer success on Data 360 and Agentforce — and a partner-focus distribution that determines who Rosetta serves first.

Partner focus from the research: 70% Systems Integrators · 15% Customers · 15% ISVs. SI practitioners are the primary user cohort by volume. Customer Trailblazers and ISV builders are second-order audiences served by the same platform.

The five ease-of-use challenges

Challenge — 01
Practitioner Scarcity
Implementation expertise concentrates in the small number of specialists who have run their third or fourth Data 360. The community below that level is large but lacks the lived experience that makes routine decisions easy. Rosetta is the channel through which specialist knowledge — encoded once — reaches the broader community at the moment of need.
Ease-of-use friction
Challenge — 02
Cognitive Cliff
Configuration decisions in Data 360 feel like one-way doors. Mapping a DLO to the wrong DMO costs a teardown, not a minor revision. Rosetta surfaces validated patterns with anti-pattern warnings and governing decision records, so the decision is made with full context rather than discovered the hard way.
Ease-of-use friction
Challenge — 03
Sales Cycle Friction
Customers buy outcomes, not platforms. Selling Data 360 in customer language requires reframing platform capabilities as business outcomes — campaigns that convert, profiles that resolve, customer service that scales. Rosetta's Three-Layer Context Artifact Model translates business intent into platform configuration.
Ease-of-use friction
Challenge — 04
Starting Point Problem
Even experienced consultants struggle with first-mile decisions. The decision tree at project kickoff has too many branches; the cost of starting in the wrong corner is high. Rosetta provides solution-mode-specific starting points and guided experiences that narrow the search space before configuration begins.
Ease-of-use friction
Challenge — 05
Community Enablement
The Salesforce content corpus is enormous. The practitioner question is not "is there content?" but "is this content current, relevant, and accurate?" Rosetta answers that question by curating a structured knowledge graph in which every recommendation cites a validated pattern, a governing ADR, and a source artifact.
Ease-of-use friction

Conversation Lifecycle & Skills Storage

Every Rosetta interaction follows a structured five-phase conversation lifecycle designed to move from vague business intent to specific, validated recommendations.

Skills storage

The conversation lifecycle is stored as a skill — a versioned, named, parameterised procedure that the agent can invoke. Skills are first-class persistent entities, not hard-coded flow logic. The current YAML-driven flow engine (see §3.2.6) becomes the runtime for the lifecycle skill; the skill definition itself lives in storage so it can be edited, versioned, audited, and extended without a code release.

A skills table in the relational store (see §3.1.5) provides:

  • Persistence — each skill has an identifier, version, definition payload, status, and ownership.
  • Memory — skills can reference scoped memory bindings (per-user, per-engagement, per-project) so a skill invocation can read and write durable state across turns.
  • Extension — new skills can be added without touching server code, on the same producer/consumer publication contract as schema content.

The Phase 1 skill inventory begins with the conversation-lifecycle skill itself plus a small number of analyst-authored diagnostic skills. [OPEN] Detailed skill schema and execution semantics to be drafted in a follow-up ADR.

Part 1 · §1.7
Knowledge Content Model

Rosetta's knowledge base consists of validated content across three categories. This is the "secret sauce" of the system — the material that distinguishes Rosetta from a generic RAG pipeline over Salesforce documentation.

ContextPatterns
63+
Validated architectural patterns organised by solution mode, phase (Discover / Design / Build / Validate / Operate), vertical, and complexity. Each pattern carries configuration hints, evidence sources, prerequisites, and governing ADRs.
Architecture Decision Records
13+
Ratified technical choices, their rationale, and the patterns they govern. ADRs provide the audit trail for why a specific configuration approach was recommended.

Vocabulary Terms. A controlled glossary bridging business language (e.g., "Golden Record") to the Salesforce semantic model, enabling synonym resolution and cross-domain term disambiguation.

Governance pipeline

Every piece of content above passes through the Constitution → ADR → DecisionTrace governance hierarchy. Raw decisions are captured in conversation as DecisionTrace nodes; once a decision point accumulates sufficient evidence (volume, coverage, consensus), a PromotionCandidate is created; on governance review the candidate is promoted to an authoritative ADR that governs one or more ContextPatterns. The three-gate pipeline is the procedural backbone that lets the canonical inventory grow without losing auditability.

Part 1 · §1.8
How Rosetta Actually Works — Four Mechanisms

Rosetta is not one system doing one mysterious thing. It is four well-understood mechanisms composed in a specific order. None of the mechanisms are novel in 2026 — the novelty is the composition, the schema they operate against, and the producer-only contract. If you understand the four mechanisms separately, the system stops being magic and becomes engineering — auditable at every step.

A critical framing point before the mechanisms: Rosetta operates inside Salesforce. It is internal tooling, not a third-party agent operating against a customer's Salesforce org from the outside. That positioning has two consequences:

  1. The knowledge Rosetta reasons over is itself governed. The canonical inventory in the graph is built from authoritative, Salesforce-published material — Trailhead modules, product documentation, partner playbooks vetted through Salesforce processes. Rosetta does not have to take a position on whether its source corpus is trustworthy; the source is governed upstream.
  2. The auditability story is about traceability, not isolation. The producer-only contract and the citation contract exist to make every recommendation traceable to a graph node and a source artifact, so a reviewer can ask "where did this come from?" and get a deterministic answer. They are not security boundaries against an untrusted customer estate.
Mechanism — 01
Structured Extraction
The LLM reads a source document — Trailhead module, implementation guide, engagement discovery dossier — with a fixed schema in front of it (for example, the DecisionTrace schema with stateAtDecision, alternativesConsidered, validation criteria). Its job is to find passages that map to those fields and emit JSON. Not "understand the document." Fill in a form. The metamodel is the schema. The LLM is the extractor. No inference about Salesforce, no judgement, no learning. A human reading the same document with the same schema printed in front of them would produce structurally the same output.
Commodity capability
Mechanism — 02
Constraint Checking
Once candidates are extracted, Rosetta does not trust them. It runs Cypher queries against the canonical inventory to ask deterministic questions: does the proposed domainKey already exist? Does the stableKey collide? Does the overrideMode declaration violate the inheritance rules in §2.5? SQL-equivalent queries against a structured graph. No learning, no probability, no inference. The LLM proposes. The constraint engine disposes.
Deterministic check
Mechanism — 03
Graph Retrieval (GraphRAG)
On every /context request, Rosetta traverses from BusinessIntent through SemanticEntity to PlatformObject / EngineFunction, collecting the canonical patterns and ADRs along the path. Deterministic, inspectable, replayable. The LLM only sees what the traversal handed it. When the system reports a finding, that finding traces back to the specific Cypher query that produced it. LinkedIn published production numbers in 2025 showing ticket resolution time dropping from 40 hours to 15 hours using essentially this pattern.
Measured technique
Mechanism — 04
Generation Under a Citation Contract
The final agent response is generated under a hard constraint: every claim points back to a node in the graph or a passage in the source corpus. The provenanceSources field exists for exactly this reason, with a canonical sourceType enum distinguishing vendor scan from metadata extract from discovery transcript from manual authoring. The citation contract is enforced at the schema level, not requested politely in a prompt.
Schema-enforced

What the LLM is not doing

This is the part worth saying out loud, because it kills the magic framing for stakeholders. The LLM is not understanding Salesforce. It is not forming architectural judgements from first principles. It is not learning from engagements — no fine-tuning, no online updating, no model drift. It is not reasoning about whether a pattern is good or bad on its merits.

The intelligence about Salesforce lives in the canonical inventory, curated by hand by the Data Foundations team and the Keenan Vision analysts working alongside them. Take the canonical inventory away and Rosetta produces nothing useful. The agent is only as smart as the graph it traverses. This is not a limitation to apologise for — it is the source of the system's auditability and the reason it can be operated against a regulated customer estate without the failure modes that plague pure-LLM agentic systems.

Why the composition is novel

Mechanisms 1–4 are commodity 2025 techniques. The novelty sits one level up. Rosetta does engagement-scoped specialisation of canonical patterns under formal override semantics, operated through a producer-only contract that never touches a customer's Salesforce org. The plugin reads a dossier and emits Cypher. The customer runs make seed-compile. That separation is what lets the system run inside Cowork without violating data sovereignty, and what lets an FDE run the same methodology across a Deloitte engagement, a Slalom engagement, and a direct customer engagement without the canonical inventory drifting in three directions.

Competitive positioning

When the question is "is Rosetta competitive with MeshMesh / Agentforce itself / the next AppExchange ISV?", the answer is not "Rosetta is smarter." The answer is — "Rosetta is governed." That is the durable position.

Why this matters for the program

Three consequences follow from the four-mechanism decomposition:

  1. Correctness is bounded by the canonical inventory's quality, not by the LLM's capability. Whatever budget is spent on model evaluation should be matched by budget for canonical inventory curation. Curation is currently concentrated in a handful of people — a single point of failure that should be treated as a program risk.
  2. The producer-only contract is the architectural decision that makes Rosetta defensible to a customer's CISO. Pressure to give Rosetta direct read access to a customer's metadata API should be resisted; the moment that line is crossed, the explainability property is lost and a class of failure modes ruled out by construction is reintroduced.
  3. The four-mechanism framing is also the right answer when somebody asks whether Rosetta is competitive with MeshMesh, with the Agentforce platform itself, or with whatever the next ISV brings to the App Exchange. The answer is not "Rosetta is smarter." The answer is "Rosetta is governed."
Part 2 · §2.1
Repository Architecture — Monorepo Consolidation

v1.6.1 split Rosetta across three repositories — rosetta-platform, rosetta-app-server, rosetta-enablement-agent — with a strict producer/consumer publication contract between them. The May 2026 review concluded this split is not paying its complexity cost:

  • Repo sprawl. Forthcoming architecture work (potential migration of the Go server to serverless functions on AWS) would produce many more small repositories under the v1.6 model.
  • Cross-repo development cost. Foundation Sprint work routinely touches all three repos at once; coordinating PRs across them slows iteration.
  • Claude Code workflow. Practical development is faster when Claude Code can see all three packages in a single workspace; the monorepo technique was developed precisely to handle this kind of multi-package coordination.

Proposed V2.0 layout

rosetta/
├── platform/      # former rosetta-platform — schema & content producer
├── app-server/    # former rosetta-app-server — Go API server
├── agent/         # former rosetta-enablement-agent — Next.js UI
├── mcp/           # new — MCP server (headless access surface)
├── shared/        # cross-package types, OpenAPI spec, schema fixtures
└── infra/         # Terraform — now targeting AWS (see §2.8)

Producer / consumer contract preserved

The producer/consumer contract from v1.6 (ADR-PL-016 / C-7) is preserved at the package level, not the repo level. The platform/ package still produces a versioned, SHA-256-pinned artifact that other packages consume; cross-package imports remain strictly one-way. The benefit is operational (one clone, one PR, one CI graph), not architectural — the constitutional invariant about producer-only publication is unchanged.

Part 2 · §2.2
Three-Layer Inheritance Architecture

Rosetta's content is organised into three inheritance layers, each owned by a different party, each with its own access-control boundary. This is the structural framework that supports engagement-scoped specialisation (§1.8) and the storage-by-owner question (§2.4).

Fig. 2.2 — Three-layer inheritance with connector boundaries

Layer mechanics

LayerOwnerContentStorageAccess
Layer 1 — Salesforce CoreSalesforceCanonical patterns, ADRs, semantic entities, platform objects, vocabularyNeo4j on platform package — served via MCP"Access = ability to connect." No user-level auth at this layer.
Layer 2 — Systems IntegratorThe SI (Deloitte, Slalom, etc.)SI-specific patterns, delivery methodologies, client templates, proprietary guidanceGoogle Drive / SharePoint indexed via connectors (Phase 1)Owned by SI; enforced by Drive / SharePoint permissions
Layer 3 — ProjectThe end customerClient-specific decisions, engagement ADRs, project patterns, canvas stateGoogle Drive / SharePoint indexed via connectors (Phase 1)Owned by customer; enforced by project permissions

Inheritance flows downward via the five-field identity contract (§2.5): a Layer 2 SI can extend or override a Layer 1 canonical pattern; a Layer 3 project can extend or override either layer above it. Retrieval at query time resolves the effective view by walking up the inheritance chain. Critically, Rosetta itself never owns or enforces access to Layer 2 or 3 content — access is granted by the file-store owner (SI or customer) and Rosetta is a consumer of that permission grant.

Part 2 · §2.3
Three-Layer Context Artifact Model

The intellectual heart of Rosetta is the Three-Layer Context Artifact Model, unchanged from v1.6.1. It is the semantic framework that bridges business intent to platform configuration. Note: this is the semantic model, not the inheritance architecture of §2.2 — the names rhyme but the concerns are different.

Layer — 01
Business Intent
Represents what a Trailblazer wants to achieve, stated in business language. Example: "Unify B2B and B2C customer profiles." BusinessIntent nodes connect to VocabularyTerm via USES_TERM edges and express themselves as SemanticEntity via EXPRESSES_AS edges.
Layer 1 of CAM
Layer — 02
Semantic Structure
The ontological middle layer containing SemanticEntity (abstract domain concepts like "Individual" or "UnifiedAccount") and ContextPattern (validated architectural patterns). ContextPattern ADDRESSES BusinessIntent, REFERENCES SemanticEntity, and RECOMMENDS EngineFunction.
Layer 2 of CAM
Layer — 03
Platform Binding
Concrete Salesforce metadata objects (PlatformObject) and engine capabilities (EngineFunction). SemanticEntity MAPS_TO PlatformObject, and PlatformObject INVOKES EngineFunction. Example: "Individual" maps to Individual__dlm, which invokes "Identity Resolution."
Layer 3 of CAM

Why this matters

The three-layer model is the key architectural differentiator: Rosetta answers questions posed in business language by traversing graph edges to concrete platform configuration objects, without hallucination, because every link in the chain is an explicitly authored, evidence-backed relationship.

Part 2 · §2.5
Five-Field Identity System & Multi-Tenancy Validation

Every inheritable node in the knowledge graph carries a five-field identity tuple (metamodel v1.6.0, §5). This is a constitutional invariant across the system and is what makes engagement-scoped specialisation (§1.8) possible.

FieldPurposeExample
domainKeyNamespace scope (root, org, project hierarchy)salesforce:rosetta-core
stableKeyImmutable identifier that survives renamespattern-cl-042
qualifiedKeyFully-qualified within domain hierarchysalesforce:rosetta-core:pattern-cl-042
displayIdHuman-readable identifierCL-042
overrideModeHow downstream domains can modify this nodeextend | suppress | replace

Inheritable labels subject to this invariant: ContextPattern, ADR, BusinessIntent, VocabularyTerm, SemanticEntity, PlatformObject, EngineFunction. Every write to a node with an inheritable label also creates a ProvenanceEvent recording the mutation.

Multi-tenancy validation requirement

The five-field identity contract was designed for the inheritance use case — engagement-scoped specialisation of canonical patterns. If Rosetta runs on a multi-tenant storage layer — whether that is a single graph with domainKey-based isolation or a per-tenant partition model — the contract must be explicitly validated against multi-tenant security and functionality requirements before architecture is frozen.

Concrete validations required before commit

1. Cross-tenant read isolation. Verify no Cypher query authored under the standard query patterns can return a node whose domainKey is outside the caller's tenant scope, even via traversal.

2. Override semantics under tenancy. Confirm that a Tenant B project overriding a ContextPattern cannot affect what Tenant A retrieves for the same pattern.

3. Provenance leakage. Verify ProvenanceEvent writes from one tenant cannot be read by another.

4. Identity uniqueness. Confirm qualifiedKey uniqueness holds globally — not just within tenant — to prevent identifier collision on cross-tenant publication to the shared directory.

[OPEN] Multi-tenancy storage model selection. Coupled with the §2.4 storage-by-owner decision.

Part 2 · §2.7
Producer / Consumer Contract

The producer/consumer contract is the foundational invariant that prevents inventory drift across engagements. The platform produces a sealed artifact; consumers replay it deterministically.

Versioning and integrity

ArtifactVersion fieldIntegrity fieldProducerConsumer
Canonical Cypher seedseedVersion (semver)SHA-256 sidecarplatform packageapp-server at make seed-compile
Engagement Cypher seedengagementSeedVersionSHA-256 sidecarplatform (plugin output)app-server on tenant bootstrap
Site Package (deployable)sitePackageVersionSHA-256 manifestplatformSI deployment tooling
Metamodel JSON SchemametamodelVersion (currently 1.5.4)Embedded $idplatformAll packages (validation)

SHA-256 sidecar protocol

Every seed file X.cypher ships with a sibling X.cypher.sha256 containing the hex digest of the seed contents. On consumer load, the digest is recomputed and compared. A mismatch halts the seed-compile step with a non-zero exit code; this is enforced by the Makefile target in app-server.

Architectural invariant

Consumers never mutate seed contents. Consumer-side state lives in MySQL/Postgres (conversation, telemetry, skills); the graph schema and canonical content are read-only from the consumer's perspective. Drift between two engagements is therefore impossible by construction — both replay the same producer artifact.

Part 2 · §2.8
AWS Deployment Topology

v1.6 specified a three-VM GCP deployment (vm-platform, vm-app, vm-agent). V2.0 supersedes this with an AWS-native topology designed for the same logical separation but using managed services where they reduce operational burden.

Intent classifier

Incoming queries pass through a lightweight intent classifier that routes the request to the right retrieval profile (pattern lookup, ADR lookup, broad knowledge, flow trigger, skill invocation). The classifier is a deterministic rule-set plus an LLM tiebreaker — never the other way around — preserving the "governed not smarter" invariant.

Retrieval engine

Hybrid retrieval combines vector similarity (against the Layer 2/3 vector index) and keyword search (BM25 against an inverted index or Postgres FTS). Results merge via Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) with a configurable k constant (default 60). A layer-priority bonus boosts Layer 1 (Salesforce Core) over Layer 2 (SI) over Layer 3 (Project) when relevance scores are within a configurable tie threshold.

/context pipeline (eight steps)

  1. Authenticate the bearer token, resolve tenant.
  2. Classify intent (pattern, ADR, broad, flow, skill).
  3. Graph traversal from Business Intent → Semantic Structure → Platform Binding (Mechanism 3).
  4. Hybrid retrieval against vector + keyword indices, RRF merge.
  5. Layer-priority re-rank using the L1/L2/L3 bonus.
  6. Constraint check (Mechanism 2) — drop candidates that violate uniqueness or override rules.
  7. Citation packaging — every chunk emitted with provenanceSources attribution.
  8. Telemetry emission — span + retrieval ledger entry written to relational DB.

Flow engine and Cypher query files

Multi-step flows (e.g., readiness assessment, promotion candidate evaluation) live as YAML definitions under app-server/flows/. Each step is either a Cypher file under app-server/cypher/ or an LLM call with a defined prompt template under app-server/prompts/. No Cypher is inlined in Go source. This is the architectural invariant from v1.6, preserved without change.

Part 3 · §3.3
agent package

The agent is the Next.js front-end and conversational orchestrator. It is the surface where users (Sellers, Implementors, Engineering) interact with Rosetta; behind it sits the app-server for retrieval and the MCP layer for external consumers.

Technology stack

Routing

Three top-level routes carry the user-facing experience: /chat/[conversationId] for conversational turns, /canvas/[artifactId] for the artifact authoring view (DecisionTrace prose, readiness narrative, Site Package preview), and /admin/skills for the skill registry UI. Authentication uses NextAuth wired to the same tenant directory as app-server.

Eleven-step GraphRAG pipeline

  1. Receive user turn; identify conversationId and tenantId.
  2. Append to messages via app-server.
  3. Call /v1/context with the turn payload.
  4. Receive ranked chunks and citation manifest.
  5. Construct prompt with cache breakpoints (see below).
  6. Call Anthropic API with streamed response.
  7. Stream tokens to the client; parse citation markers in-flight.
  8. Validate citation contract: every claim must point to a provenanceSources entry.
  9. If a claim lacks attribution, redact and emit a graceful fallback.
  10. Persist completed turn to messages with retrieval ledger backref.
  11. Emit OTEL span with cost, latency, and citation count attributes.

Hybrid retrieval and domain-aware CRUD

The agent surfaces a domain-aware CRUD view for authorized users: ContextPatterns and ADRs are editable through forms whose schema is derived directly from the metamodel JSON Schemas exposed by app-server. Edits are written through the producer/consumer contract — they become engagement seed contributions, not direct graph mutations.

Canvas

The Canvas is a long-running editable surface anchored to a conversation. It is where readiness narratives, DecisionTrace prose, and Site Package previews materialize. Canvas content streams from the same /v1/context pipeline; the difference from chat is that the surface persists across turns and supports user edits that re-enter the prompt for the next turn.

Prompt architecture with cache breakpoints

Prompts are assembled in five segments to maximize Anthropic prompt-caching: (1) system identity — Rosetta operator role and citation contract; (2) metamodel snippet — the relevant subset of v1.5.4 schemas for the current intent; (3) retrieved chunks — the ranked context block from /v1/context; (4) conversation history; (5) current user turn. Cache breakpoints sit between (1)–(2), (2)–(3), and (4)–(5), so the stable head of every prompt is reused across turns within a conversation.

Part 3 · §3.4
Slack — Primary Interaction Plane

Slack is Rosetta's primary practitioner-facing surface. Sellers, Implementors, and Engineering interact with Rosetta in Slack workflows and Agentforce channels, not in a separate web UI. The Slack integration is a thin wrapper that delegates every meaningful operation to the MCP server (see §3.5).

Why Slack first

The practitioners Rosetta serves spend their workday in Slack — channels for account teams, project channels for delivery, Agentforce DMs for in-flow assistance. Building a separate destination application introduces switching cost and rarely captures real usage. By making Slack the primary plane, Rosetta meets practitioners where they already are.

Architecture — wraps the MCP server

The Slack surface is a presentation adapter over the MCP server. It accepts user input from slash commands, mentions, and message shortcuts; translates each interaction into one or more MCP tool calls; and renders the responses as Slack Block Kit messages. No retrieval logic, no graph access, no skill execution lives in the Slack layer — every call is delegated.

Authentication and tenancy

Slack workspace ↔ Rosetta tenant binding is established at installation. Each Slack user is mapped to a Rosetta caller identity through the tenant directory; bearer tokens are issued per workspace, not per user, and per-user identity is carried in the MCP call envelope. Every interaction is logged to mcp_audit the same way as any other MCP call — there is no parallel Slack-side audit ledger.

Architectural invariant

The Slack package contains no business logic. It is a presentation layer over MCP. Every retrieval, every skill invocation, every citation, every audit entry flows through the MCP server. If a feature can't be expressed as a sequence of MCP tool calls, it does not belong in the Slack surface — it belongs in the MCP server (and from there is available to every other consumer too).

Authentication

The MCP server reuses the rsk_-prefixed bearer token scheme from v1.6 for the agent. A single token authenticates a tenant + caller identity; every tool call is logged to mcp_audit with the invoked tool name, input schema digest, latency, and outcome. There is no separate identity system for MCP — the rule is one tenant directory, one token scheme, one audit ledger.

Architectural invariant

The MCP layer is a protocol adapter, not a parallel implementation. Every MCP tool delegates to an existing app-server endpoint. There is no business logic in mcp — only request shaping, audit logging, and response packaging. This preserves the citation contract and the producer/consumer separation.

Part 5
Glossary

Canonical definitions for terms used throughout this specification.

TermDefinition
ADRArchitecture Decision Record. A short, dated document capturing one decision, its context, alternatives considered, and consequences. Rosetta has two series: ADR-PL (platform) and ADR-GO (consumer service).
Canonical InventoryThe hand-curated graph of ContextPatterns, ADRs, relationships, and DecisionTraces maintained by the Data Foundations team. The source of truth Rosetta retrieves against.
Citation ContractThe schema-level rule that every claim in a generated artifact must point back to a node in the canonical inventory or a passage in the engagement dossier, via provenanceSources.
ContextPatternA named, versioned design pattern stored as a node in Neo4j. The canonical inventory currently contains 63+ patterns across the eight metamodel domains.
ConsumerThe runtime side of Rosetta: app-server, agent, and mcp. Consumes producer artifacts; never writes back to canonical content.
CypherNeo4j's query language. Rosetta's producer artifact format and the language of every graph query in app-server.
DecisionTraceA first-class node type recording stateAtDecision, alternativesConsidered, and validation criteria. The trail of why a pattern was authored or overridden.
Engagement SeedThe plugin output: engagement-scoped Cypher that extends or overrides canonical patterns for one customer engagement. SHA-256 signed; replayed by the consumer at make seed-compile.
Five-Field IdentityThe identity contract for any inheritable graph node: domainKey, stableKey, tenantId, layer, overrideMode. Validated before any graph write.
GraphRAGRetrieval pattern that combines graph traversal with hybrid retrieval; the candidates surfaced to the LLM are determined by Cypher paths, not by similarity alone.
Layer 1 / 2 / 3The three inheritance layers: Salesforce Core (canonical, Neo4j+MCP), SI (Drive/SharePoint, system integrator content), Project (Drive/SharePoint, engagement-specific content).
MCPModel Context Protocol. The protocol by which external clients (Claude.ai, Slack, ISV tools) consume Rosetta tools without going through the agent UI. See §3.5.
Override ModeOne of extend, refine, supersede. Declares how an engagement seed modifies a canonical pattern; validated by Mechanism 2 constraint checks.
ProducerThe authoring side of Rosetta: the platform package. Owns the canonical inventory, the engagement plugin, and the seed-compile pipeline. Never observes consumer runtime state.
ProvenanceSourceThe enumerated source-type field attached to every claim and node: vendor_scan, metadata_extract, discovery_transcript, manual_authoring. Backbone of the citation contract.
Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF)The merge algorithm used to combine vector and keyword retrieval rankings. Score = sum over rankers of 1/(k + rank). Default k=60.
Site PackageA signed, deployable bundle containing canonical seed + engagement seed + metamodel + prompts, produced by platform for SI consumption.
SkillA persistent, registered capability package (ZIP) that a tenant has installed. Owned by Rosetta in V2.0 (moved inside from Claude in the Step 4 architecture). Logged via skill_invocations; can carry per-tenant memory.
TenantAn isolated namespace for a customer or engagement. All Layer 2/3 content, conversations, telemetry, and skills are tenant-scoped.
