01

What we learned from four inputs

The brief pointed at concrete things rather than a blank page: an existing Bubble app to mine for functionality, a YouTube channel to mine for content, a live site to mine for UI, and — added after first review — an adjacent product to mine for feature inspiration. Each shaped a different layer of this spec.

Inspiring Excellence (the Bubble export)

This is a working product, not a mockup — 46 pages and 13 data types, with real workflows for sign-up/login, content approval, tagging, and a personal library. The concepts worth carrying forward:

  • Session → Insight. Every piece of content is one record with a title, an SME, tags, a summary, and engagement counters (plays, shares).
  • SME as a first-class profile, not just a byline — bio, employer, title, social links, and their own list of Insights (the "SME sessions" screenshot).
  • A personal library built from one join table (user_module): Later, Favourite, and Watched are just three booleans on the same row per user-per-insight — cheap to replicate, and it's exactly what the "LATER / FAVE / HISTORY" icon row in the screenshots does.
  • Content tiers (level_text: Public / Registered / Paid) — a gating model already designed in, even if unused today.
  • An approval queue before content goes live (s-session-approve, s-sme-approve) — new SMEs and new sessions are reviewed, not auto-published.
  • No-login share pages for sessions, SMEs, and tags — content is shareable without forcing a sign-up wall.

Performance Intelligence (the YouTube channel)

The channel, run by performance coach Jez Cartwright, anchors on long-form conversations — CEO and founder interviews, leadership and high-performance coaching. That's the "MasterClass" half of the brief: substantial, expert-led, sit-down content rather than quick tips. The channel is the entire content operation — there's no separate video CMS to build; YouTube is the asset store, and this app is a curated viewing layer on top of it.

Weekly Briefing (the UI reference)

Also a Jez Cartwright product, so it's a natural sibling rather than an arbitrary style reference — and one seen directly, not just described. The masthead is a full-width orange band: a small π mark above "PERFORMANCE INTELLIGENCE / WEEKLY BRIEFING" in a wide-tracked serif, with the day's date pinned top-left, framing it as a dated briefing rather than an evergreen library. What reads as "navigation" is actually a row of topic pills — Leadership, Markets, Psychology, Technology, Geopolitics, Philosophy — each carrying its own accent colour rather than one universal orange; account, notes, and messages live instead behind three small icons tucked in the top-right corner. Each item below is numbered, sits against a thin orange rail, and splits into a bold headline + grey subhead, then a two-column body — "Why it matters" prose with a cited source link, beside an italic "Key insight" pull-quote — with a heart (save) and an orange × (dismiss) per item. Section 07 carries this pattern through in detail.

Audvisor (adjacent, for inspiration)

Not a video product — a micro-podcast app built around "Brilliance in 3 minutes": thousands of sub-3-minute audio lessons from named experts (Tom Peters, Josh Bersin, and others among them), priced as a $120/year subscription after a free trial. It's the closest live comparable to the Shorts half of this app, and three things from it are worth borrowing:

  • Duration as the pitch, not an apology. Audvisor markets brevity itself — "Brilliance in 3 minutes" — rather than treating short content as a lesser version of the long-form. Worth carrying that framing into how the Discover feed is positioned, not just how it's built.
  • Editorial playlists above raw tags. Content is grouped into named, curated shelves — Strategy & Execution, Leadership, Mental Health — sitting above simple tag filtering as a guided "start here" layer. See the new Curated Collections screen in section 04.
  • Sharing with a person, not just a link. Audvisor's "My Chats" lets someone send a specific episode into a conversation with a colleague, and its MentorCloud partnership uses the same mechanism for a mentor to point a mentee at one piece of content. A lighter version of this — share one Insight to a named person with a note — is worth adding to the Insight detail screen and Messages, rather than building a full public share link as the only option.

Audvisor also curates against a stated quality bar it calls T.R.A.C.K. — Timeless, Relevant, Actionable, Curated, Knowledge. That's a usable checklist for the Admin approval step (section 09) when deciding what gets published, and separately, what gets featured in a Collection.

02

Product concept

One content model, two ways of watching it.

Every video in the app is an Insight: one SME, a set of topic tags, and a home in the personal library. What changes is the format it was published in on YouTube, and the way the app lets you watch it:

Shorts

≤3 minutes, vertical. Surfaced in a full-screen, swipe-to-advance feed — autoplay, loop, one Insight at a time. This is the TikTok half: low-friction discovery, built for a scroll session.

Long-form

Interviews and deep dives. Surfaced as a browsable library with a proper detail page — SME byline, summary, tags, related Insights. This is the MasterClass half: something you choose deliberately and sit with.

Saving, favouriting, sharing, and watch history work identically across both — a user builds one library regardless of which format they watched something in.

06

YouTube ingestion pipeline

YouTube is the video store. The app only ever stores metadata and an embed reference — never the video file.

  1. Poll the channel's uploads. YouTube Data API v3 against the channel's uploads playlist (PubSubHubbub push notification as an optional faster path) for new videos on @performance-intelligence.
  2. Classify format automatically. Duration ≤ ~180 seconds and vertical aspect ratio (or presence on the channel's Shorts shelf) → Shorts; everything else → Long-form.
  3. Pull metadata only — title, description, duration, publish date, thumbnail, auto-captions (for search and the transcript panel). The videoId is the only pointer stored to the actual media.
  4. Land in the Admin queue. New uploads sit as drafts until an editor assigns an SME, confirms tags, and writes the short summary — this mirrors the Bubble app's approval workflow rather than auto-publishing.
  5. Play back via YouTube's embedded player (IFrame API on web, YouTube's iOS player SDK / WKWebView on iOS) — never a downloaded or re-hosted file.
Risk to validate early YouTube's terms require its player chrome, branding, and (where applicable) ads to stay visible. A fully "clean," silent, edge-to-edge TikTok feel may be constrained by that, and autoplay-with-sound is restricted on iOS Safari/WebView. Worth a short technical spike against the real embed before committing to the Discover feed's exact interaction model.

09

Admin & moderation

Nothing from YouTube goes live untouched — this is the one workflow carried over wholesale from the Bubble app.

  • Sync queue — new uploads appear as drafts, tagged with the auto-detected format.
  • Approve Insight — assign or confirm SME, confirm tags, write the short-form summary, set access tier, publish.
  • Approve SME — new expert profiles are reviewed before they're publicly listed.
  • Curate a Collection — separately from publishing, an editor can pull an approved Insight into a named shelf; the suggested bar is Audvisor's T.R.A.C.K. — Timeless, Relevant, Actionable, Curated, Knowledge — so Collections stay a genuine "best of," not everything that was published.
  • Engagement dashboard — plays, shares, and searches per Insight and per tag, the same counters the Bubble app already tracked.
10

Non-functional requirements

  • Performance — pre-fetch the next 1–2 Shorts in the feed so swipe-to-advance feels instant.
  • Accessibility — captions on by default in the Shorts feed; transcripts available on every long-form Insight; Dynamic Type support on iOS.
  • Compliance — no downloading or offline caching of video, per YouTube's Terms of Service; player branding stays visible.
  • Privacy — account deletion and unsubscribe, matching what Weekly Briefing already offers.
  • Analytics parity — plays, shares, and searches tracked per Insight and per tag from day one, not bolted on later.

Phase 3
Extend

Push and assist

Push notifications for new SME uploads, offline note sync, and an AI Q&A layer over Insight transcripts — revisiting the unused ai table found in the Bubble export, worked through in full as its own extended spec: The Wisdom Layer.

12

Open questions

  • Naming and branding — is this a Weekly Briefing sibling, a Performance Intelligence-branded app, or its own identity? This spec deliberately doesn't invent a name.
  • Access tiers and pricing — keep the Public/Registered/Paid model from Bubble, or make everything free since the source content is already public on YouTube? If a Paid tier ships, Audvisor's $120/year is a live benchmark for a comparable micro-content subscription.
  • Curation ownership — does Jez personally approve new Insights and pick Collections, or does an editor run the Admin queue day to day?
  • Accounts — same login as Weekly Briefing, or a separate account system?
  • B2B / mentoring angle — Audvisor's MentorCloud partnership lets a mentor push specific content to a mentee. Is a coaching/enterprise use case in scope here, or is "share with a note" purely a peer-to-peer, consumer-app feature?
  • iOS build approach — confirm React Native is workable against whatever team/skillset will actually build this, versus native Swift.
13

Self-critique

Before handing this over: where this spec is solid, and where it's guessing.

Solid The screen list, data model, and admin workflow aren't invented — they're read directly off a real, working Bubble app (46 pages, 13 data types), so the functional scope has precedent rather than being a generic "TikTok meets MasterClass" guess.
Corrected The first draft of this spec built section 07 from a fetched text description of weeklybriefing.jezcartwright.com, because an automated screenshot was blocked by this environment's network policy. Ian then supplied a real screenshot, which corrected two real misses — the "nav" is a colour-coded topic-pill row, not a text menu, and utilities sit behind corner icons — both now reflected in sections 01, 03, and 07. Still not seen directly: what's actually behind those corner icons (My Notes, Messages, account), so those three screens in section 04 remain functional inference, not a direct look.
Weaker I didn't get an actual list of the channel's videos (titles, durations, current Shorts-vs-long-form split) — only the channel's About description. The ingestion pipeline in section 06 is designed against YouTube's general API capabilities, not against this channel's real current catalogue. Worth confirming the Shorts/long-form mix before sizing the Discover feed's content pool.
Weaker The Bubble export shows signs of being a working, slightly messy production app — fields marked "(notused)," leftover demo-template auth comments, an unrelated ratings module bundled in. I used judgment to separate signal from noise; worth checking my read of it against the live app if one is still running, rather than trusting this document alone.
Scope This is a product spec, not a technical design doc — the React Native / thin-backend recommendation in section 08 is a reasonable default, not a committed architecture decision.
Weaker The Audvisor findings (Collections, T.R.A.C.K., "share with a note") come from its marketing site and one partner press release — its library page 404'd, so I never saw its actual browse/playlist UI directly. Treat those additions as directionally sound inspiration, not a reverse-engineered feature set the way the Bubble app's sections are.
